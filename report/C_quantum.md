# Track Report: C_quantum (Quantum Optimization: QAOA, VQE, QNN & Annealing Limits)

## 1. Problem Statement & Inefficiency
- **Domain & Process**: Variational quantum algorithms (VQA) and hybrid quantum-classical optimization applied to combinatorial optimization (QAOA for Max-Cut), quantum chemistry / condensed matter physics (VQE for molecular $H_2$ and Transverse-Field Ising Models), and quantum machine learning (PQC classifiers).
- **Observed Bottlenecks & Inefficiencies**:
  1. *Barren Plateaus*: In parameterized quantum circuits forming approximate Haar 2-designs, the gradient variance of global cost functions vanishes exponentially with the qubit count ($\text{Var} \sim 2^{-n}$), rendering gradient descent intractable on deep architectures.
  2. *Quantum Projection (Shot) Noise*: On physical hardware, statevector amplitudes are inaccessible; observables must be estimated from finite measurement shots $S$, introducing statistical variance scaling as $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{S})$.
  3. *Hardware Noise & Decoherence*: Real NISQ devices suffer from gate infidelities ($0.1\%$ single-qubit, $1\%$ two-qubit) and state-preparation-and-measurement (SPAM) readout errors ($1\text{--}2\%$), which bias energy estimates and degrade quantum entanglement.
  4. *Quantum Annealing Embedding Bottlenecks*: Physical quantum annealers have sparse connectivity graphs (Chimera, Pegasus), requiring minor embedding with physical qubit chains, chain breaks, and severe dynamic range compression.
- **Baseline Description**:
  - QAOA: Compared against brute-force global optimum cut ($15.0$) from Track B.
  - VQE: Compared against exact classical matrix diagonalization (`numpy.linalg.eigh`).
  - Quantum Gradients: Classical parameter-shift Euclidean baseline vs. stochastic SPSA vs. Riemannian Quantum Natural Gradient (QNG).

## 2. Mathematical Formulations

### 2.1 Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA)
For a graph $G = (V, E)$ with Max-Cut Ising Hamiltonian $H_C = \sum_{(u,v) \in E} J_{uv} Z_u Z_v + \text{offset} \cdot I$, the $p$-layer variational state is:
$$|\psi(\vec{\gamma}, \vec{\beta})\rangle = \prod_{l=1}^p \left( e^{-i \beta_l H_B} e^{-i \gamma_l H_C} \right) |+\rangle^{\otimes n}$$
where $H_B = \sum_{j=1}^n X_j$ is the transverse mixer Hamiltonian. The expected cut is maximized by minimizing:
$$\langle H_C(\vec{\gamma}, \vec{\beta}) \rangle = \langle \psi(\vec{\gamma}, \vec{\beta}) | H_C | \psi(\vec{\gamma}, \vec{\beta}) \rangle, \qquad \langle \text{Cut} \rangle = -\langle H_C \rangle$$
The approximation ratio is defined as $\alpha = \frac{\langle \text{Cut} \rangle}{\text{Cut}^*}$.

### 2.2 Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE)
VQE exploits the Rayleigh-Ritz variational principle to estimate the ground state energy $E_0$:
$$E_0 \le \min_{\vec{\theta}} \langle H \rangle_{\vec{\theta}} = \langle \psi(\vec{\theta}) | H | \psi(\vec{\theta}) \rangle$$
- **Molecular $H_2$ Hamiltonian** ($R = 0.735\text{ \AA}$, STO-3G parity reduction):
  $$H_{H_2} = -1.052373 II + 0.397937 ZI - 0.397937 IZ - 0.011280 ZZ + 0.180931 XX$$
- **2-Qubit Transverse-Field Ising Model (TFIM)** ($J=1.0, g=0.8$):
  $$H_{\text{TFIM}} = -1.0 ZZ - 0.8 IX - 0.8 XI$$

### 2.3 Quantum Gradient Estimation Methods
1. **Parameter-Shift Rule (PSR)**:
   For gates generated by Pauli generators $G = e^{-i \frac{\theta}{2} P}$, the exact analytical gradient is:
   $$\frac{\partial \langle H \rangle}{\partial \theta_k} = \frac{\langle H \rangle_{\theta_k + \frac{\pi}{2}} - \langle H \rangle_{\theta_k - \frac{\pi}{2}}}{2}$$
   Requires $2D$ circuit evaluations per gradient step ($D$ parameters).
2. **Simultaneous Perturbation Stochastic Approximation (SPSA)**:
   Random Bernoulli perturbation $\Delta \in \{-1, +1\}^D$:
   $$g_k = \frac{\mathcal{L}(\theta + c_t \Delta) - \mathcal{L}(\theta - c_t \Delta)}{2 c_t \Delta_k}$$
   Requires exactly $2$ circuit evaluations per step, independent of parameter dimension $D$.
3. **Quantum Natural Gradient (QNG)**:
   Preconditions the Euclidean gradient with the Fubini-Study metric tensor $F$:
   $$F_{kl}(\vec{\theta}) = \text{Re} \left[ \langle \partial_k \psi | \partial_l \psi \rangle - \langle \partial_k \psi | \psi \rangle \langle \psi | \partial_l \psi \rangle \right]$$
   Update rule: $\vec{\theta}_{t+1} = \vec{\theta}_t - \eta (F + \epsilon I)^{-1} \nabla \mathcal{L}(\vec{\theta}_t)$.

### 2.4 Barren Plateaus Scaling
For an ensemble of circuits forming an approximate 2-design over the unitary group $U(2^n)$:
$$\text{Var}_{\vec{\theta}} \left[ \frac{\partial \langle H_{\text{global}} \rangle}{\partial \theta_k} \right] \in \mathcal{O}\left( \frac{1}{2^n} \right)$$
when $H_{\text{global}} = Z^{\otimes n}$, whereas local observables $H_{\text{local}} = Z_0$ preserve polynomial gradient variance $\mathcal{O}(1/\text{poly}(n))$.

## 6. Limits of Embedding QUBO on Quantum Annealing Hardware

While gate-based QPUs execute parameterized unitary transformations, quantum annealers (e.g., D-Wave Advantage and 2000Q) minimize classical Ising spin Hamiltonians by adiabatically evolving a transverse-field driver into a target problem Hamiltonian:
$$H(s) = -(1 - s) \sum_i \Delta_i X_i + s \left[ \sum_i h_i Z_i + \sum_{(i, j)} J_{ij} Z_i Z_j \right]$$

Embedding real-world QUBO problems onto physical annealing hardware faces severe physical and mathematical limitations:

### 6.1 Sparse Physical Topology vs. Dense Problem Graphs
- **Graph Degree Mismatch**: Physical QPUs have limited connectivity. D-Wave Chimera graphs have qubit degree 6 (bipartite $K_{4,4}$ cells); the newer Pegasus topology has degree 15; Zephyr topology has degree 20.
- **Clique Embedding ($K_n$) Explosion**: Combinatorial optimization problems (e.g., dense Max-Cut, TSP, or knapsack with quadratic penalties $\sum w_i x_i$) require dense all-to-all quadratic couplings $K_n$.
- Embedding an $n$-node complete graph onto a sparse physical grid requires **minor embedding**, where a single logical variable is mapped to a connected chain of physical qubits. On Chimera graphs, embedding $K_n$ requires $\mathcal{O}(n^2)$ physical qubits. For example, embedding a fully connected graph of just $N=64$ logical variables consumes over 1,000 physical qubits.

### 6.2 Ferromagnetic Coupling, Chain Breaks, and Energy Distortion
- To force physical qubits in chain $C_u$ to behave as a single logical spin $s_u$, strong ferromagnetic couplings $-J_{\text{chain}} Z_i Z_j$ ($J_{\text{chain}} > 0$) are applied between adjacent physical qubits in the chain.
- **Dynamic Range Compression**: Hardware couplers operate within a bounded physical range (typically $J \in [-1.0, +1.0]$ and $h \in [-2.0, +2.0]$). Setting $J_{\text{chain}} \ge 2 \max |J_{uv}|$ forces all logical problem couplings $J_{uv}$ to be scaled down by a factor of $1 / J_{\text{chain}}$.
- **Loss of Energy Resolution**: Scaling down the logical Hamiltonian compresses the energy gap $\Delta E$ between the ground state and excited states. The compressed energy differences frequently fall below the analog control error (ICE) and thermal noise threshold ($k_B T \approx 12\text{--}15\text{ mK} \approx 1\text{ GHz}$), destroying ground-state fidelity.
- **Chain Breaks**: When thermal fluctuations or non-adiabatic transitions overcome $J_{\text{chain}}$, physical qubits within the same logical chain disagree (e.g., some read $+1$, others $-1$). Resolving chain breaks requires classical heuristics (e.g., majority voting or weighted averaging), which introduces post-processing bias and voids global optimality guarantees.

### 6.3 Analog Control Precision and Precision Noise
- Unlike digital gate-based quantum circuits, quantum annealers specify coupler biases via digital-to-analog converters (DACs) with only $4\text{--}5$ bits of effective resolution (quantization error $\sim 2\text{--}3\%$).
- Minor-embedded Hamiltonians with delicate penalty terms (such as knapsack capacity penalties $P(\sum w_i x_i - W)^2$) become severely distorted by DAC quantization and $1/f$ flux noise, leading the physical annealer to return infeasible bitstrings that violate constraints.
